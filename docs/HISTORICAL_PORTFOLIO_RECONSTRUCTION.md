# Historical portfolio reconstruction (local implementation; not deployed)

Portfolio history now has two distinct authorities. `arbor_portfolio_snapshots` retains immutable observations: what Arbor captured from the records and live prices available at that time. `arbor_reconstructed_portfolio_history()` derives a canonical series from the owner's *current corrected dated ledger* and separately cached historical source observations. It does not write derived values or alter old snapshots. Every returned point identifies `origin` (`observed` or `reconstructed`), and each reconstructed point carries its actual source dates, timestamps and provenance. The API also carries the first dated investment date so the chart can disclose when complete source coverage begins later. A segment number prevents the chart from drawing a continuous line across unavailable dates.

## Sources and valuation

- Marketstack Basic `/v2/eod`: exact VT, VGT and BND USD closes. A normal trading day requires its own EOD close. A confirmed exchange closure or weekend may use a verified prior close no more than four calendar days old. Actual source date is retained.
- BSP official [Philippine Peso per US Dollar daily archive](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/statistics/external/pesodollar.xlsx): exact daily USD/PHP rate, currently extending back to 1978. The adapter reads the published spreadsheet date cells and normalizes Excel serialization artifacts to its four-decimal displayed precision. Missing cells remain missing; a weekend or confirmed nonpublishing day may use a prior published rate no more than four calendar days old. No future rate or interpolation. BSP is the source of the historical FX data. This derivative portfolio valuation is not a BSP publication; the underlying BSP series is freely available from BSP. No BSP logo is used.
- Coinranking BTC/PHP price history: a genuine observation on the valuation date, at or before the UTC day cutoff. The selected timestamp is retained. A future nearest observation is never accepted. The current arrangement exposes at most the supported one-year historical window; unsupported dates remain unavailable. Arbor does not redistribute a raw Coinranking data set.
- TOAP / UITF.com.ph NAVPU for the six exact supported ATRAM/BPI fund classes: the founder has authorized a one-time historical read and prospective capture from the already-authorized daily pages. The shared cache stores only actual source-date NAVPU, exact fund/class identity, PHP currency and TOAP provenance. Normal weekdays need a NAV for that date; weekends or operator-confirmed nonpublishing dates may use a verified prior NAV at most four calendar days old. An unconfirmed missing weekday is not bridged. A missing NAV, unknown units, or undated opening position still makes complete reconstruction unavailable; current/manual fund value and recorded cost are never projected backward.

Daily reconstructed portfolio dates are not new market observations. For example, a Sunday point can use Friday's actual ETF close, BSP rate and fund NAV while BTC uses a genuine Sunday observation. The point records Sunday as valuation date and Friday as the ETF/FX/NAV source dates. Prior-source use may not bridge an unconfirmed missing weekday, even when the final valuation day itself is a confirmed holiday. Missing expected trading-day ETF data, missing FX for a held USD asset, missing historical fund NAV, and undated opening units all prevent a purported complete reconstructed total. Compatible genuine snapshots may remain as observed checkpoints; a snapshot made obsolete by a later ledger correction remains stored but is excluded from the canonical chart.

The SQL engine replays active dated entries per holding, including archived zero-unit holdings, and calculates PHP value, native ETF USD value, complete recorded PHP cost, and gain with PostgreSQL `numeric`. A contribution changes units/cost; it is never itself gain. Old snapshot cost and FX stay null when they were not captured. No transaction date creates a market observation. A ledger-change trigger records the earliest affected date, including the old date on a move, while the derived series is rebuilt on each owner read rather than maintained as a fragile owner cache.

## Security, import and rollout

Shared historical observations and confirmed nonpublishing days are operator-only tables, not owner financial data. Authenticated users cannot read/write those shared tables or directly mutate snapshots or change markers. The security-definer history RPC takes the owner from `auth.uid()`, and the status view is security-invoker. Factual history remains core tracking; the endpoint does not include Plus planning/insight payloads.

The historical ETF/BSP/BTC importer is **not** wired to app startup or cron. It requires both `--write` and `ARBOR_HISTORICAL_CACHE_WRITE_ENABLED=true`, and writes only shared market observations through the established operator cache path. The normal TOAP daily refresh now appends its already-retrieved, exactly matched NAVPU to that same shared table; it makes no additional TOAP request. Same-date NAV conflicts are rejected by a database trigger rather than overwritten. Neither path modifies an owner's ledger or snapshots. The importers have not been run against hosted Supabase. Holidays also require separately verified operator records; an absent weekday quote is never silently treated as a holiday.

TOAP's historical detail page has a CAPTCHA-gated date filter and offers a CSV export only after the filter. The founder's source-use authorization does not remove this access gate. `python -m app.market_data.nav_history_cli DIRECTORY --validate-only` checks exactly six **NAV-only** `product_id.csv` files with `fund_name,date,navpu` columns, exact class names, ISO source dates, and the approved 15,635 per-class total without writing. `--write-local` additionally requires `ARBOR_LOCAL_NAV_IMPORT=1` and the disposable `127.0.0.1:55444/arbor_nav_test` database. The one-time production `--write-hosted --project-ref PROJECT_REF` path requires `ARBOR_HOSTED_NAV_IMPORT=1`, an exact project URL match, and an operator-only shared-cache credential; it writes source observations only, in bounded batches, with same-date conflicting NAV values rejected by the database. Neither mode fetches the gated endpoint or alters owner snapshots, holdings, or investment entries. The six human-exported source reports supplied for qualification contained 15,635 unique source-date NAVPU rows across the exact classes. Source-reported weekend dates remain dated source observations, not generated Arbor carry-forward rows. ROI/YTD columns and raw page HTML are rejected/not persisted. Keep the source CSV files outside Git.

The additive migration is `backend/migrations/20260930130000_reconstructed_portfolio_history.sql`, after the already-applied snapshot-context migration. It now also accepts only the six canonical TOAP NAV keys, stores NAV class metadata, and guards same-date conflict. The old application ignores its new tables, trigger and RPC and remains compatible with the new schema. The new reader and prospective NAV writer require the new table/RPC, so **migration-first** is the only compatible order. Nothing has been applied to hosted Supabase. A non-destructive rollback is to redeploy the prior application while retaining the additive tables and immutable snapshots; dropping those tables would lose provenance and is not the default rollback.

## Current limits

The owner RPC rebuilds daily values without synchronous vendor calls. It owns chronological pagination in bounded pages of at most 1,000 points, avoiding an unordered outer PostgREST range. This is deterministic and avoids stale owner-derived caches, but replay work still scales with elapsed days and holdings; larger accounts should be benchmarked before production rollout. Marketstack and Coinranking plan/source retention can be narrower than BSP's archive, so `All` starts at the first *completely supportable* date, not necessarily the first transaction. An undated opening position has no invented acquisition date. Legacy snapshots cannot prove every kind of pre-migration holding deletion, so uncertain historical compatibility should be treated conservatively during release review.
